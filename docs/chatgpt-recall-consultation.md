## Executive verdict

- The gap is a semantic extraction generalisation failure, not a fundamental limitation of the statutory decision graph — but verify that conclusion through an oracle test before committing further engineering effort.
- Decision table: replace regex-gated extraction with a supervised multilingual classifier; regex = high-precision supplementary evidence only; train the existing 8,000-record corpus before expanding indiscriminately; prefer a multilingual encoder over English-first ModernBERT-large; retain full-question + clause-level fusion, with corrections; regularised per-head calibration; independent human-authored multilingual holdout for evaluation; 70% exact feasibility = plausible, but not established; Graph v4 stays frozen; biggest risk = memorising a new synthetic grammar instead of the original test bank.
- "The 65-percentage-point difference between Brutal-230 and Fresh-20 is the most important signal in this brief. Performance on known wording is no longer a reliable indicator of progress."

## 1. Hybrid vs replacement — replace, keep a limited hybrid evidence layer

- Do not retain the existing regex-anchor architecture. The classifier must independently detect every supported issue and fact, whether or not a recognised keyword appears.
- Flow: question → Unicode normalisation + contextual clause segmentation → multilingual classifier (11 issue heads; 28 fact heads + enums) + regex evidence (explicit references, dates, orders, amounts, lexical evidence) → evidence reconciliation (scope · negation · entity roles · time · conflicts; no missing-anchor veto) → Graph v4 (frozen) → prose renderer (frozen).
- Ownership: issue requested / implicit intent / semantic facts / negation & hypothetical scope / claimant & opposing-party roles → classifier. Explicit dates & monetary amounts → deterministic parser. Explicit statutory references → regex parser. Conflicting evidence → reconciliation layer. Applicable sections → Graph v4. "A statutory reference is evidence of a mention, not automatically evidence that the corresponding remedy was requested."
- The logic `if not regex_anchor_matches(text): return False` must disappear completely. Conceptually: prediction = classifier.predict(text); evidence = parser.extract(text); resolved = reconcile(prediction, evidence, preserve_semantic_recall=True); return graph_v4(resolved). "Regex absence must be treated as no additional evidence, not negative evidence."
- Acceptance: all 39 extraction heads operate independently of regex anchors (`src/laya_v7_classifier.py`); all graph-consumed facts have defined producers; zero classifier positives suppressed solely by missing anchors; anchorless paraphrase diagnostic requested-issue recall ≥90%; graph contract diagnostic 100% of graph input keys covered. "The eight dead fact keys must be repaired before the first meaningful training run. A larger encoder cannot recover a fact that the extraction interface never produces."

## 2. Paraphrase augmentation — ranked

"Increasing corpus size without increasing semantic and linguistic diversity will produce diminishing returns." Rank: 1. LLM-generated semantic paraphrases (very high); 2. hard-negative mining from classifier errors (very high after first training); 3. controlled semantic perturbations (high); 4. English–Kannada back-translation (moderate); 5. more variations of existing templates (low–moderate).

- LLM paraphrase pipeline: generate from abstract legal scenarios, NOT by rewriting template sentences. (1) Statutory scenario specification (independent facts, roles, remedies, temporal states); (2) natural question generation (layman narratives, indirect requests, emotional language, incomplete sentences); (3) independent label extraction (re-extract issues, facts, roles, spans from generated text); (4) semantic consistency verification (reject label drift, ambiguous references, contradictory facts); (5) deduplication + grouped partitioning (keep scenario families and paraphrase siblings together); (6) approved training corpus with provenance, generator version, labels, verification status. Example: scenario {"custody_claim": true, "minor_child": true, "divorce_asked": false, "residence_claim": false} → "My husband took our daughter to his parents' house. I want her to come and stay with me. I am not planning to end the marriage." Label correctness at scale: generate from independent scenario specs, never from sealed questions; independent verification pass, not the generating model's labels; recompute evidence spans after every paraphrase; reject intent/negation/role/temporal changes; manually audit a stratified sample of ≥300 records, targeting ≥98% label correctness and no critical polarity or claimant-role errors; use only generation models/services whose licences/terms permit the training use.
- What will NOT generalise (artefact → wrong learning): fixed sentence openings → issue identity from sentence position; same legal vocabulary → keyword dependence disguised as semantic learning; fixed issue ordering → first issue always more important; identical gender distribution → gender as shortcut for legal intent; repeated negation structures → negation from a single word rather than scope; statutory terms in every positive → failure on layman descriptions; clean Kannada translations → failure on natural colloquial Kannada; template-specific punctuation → artificial clause boundaries; identical fact combinations → spurious dependencies between unrelated issues. "The worst outcome would be replacing a bank-tuned regex system with a template-tuned neural classifier."
- Hard-negative mining: after the first training run, use out-of-fold predictions on the training corpus + independently generated challenge examples. Prioritise: confidently missed requested issues; mentioned-vs-requested confusion; wrong claimant roles/property ownership; incorrect negation scope; multi-issue suppression; false activation on unrelated questions. "Do not repeatedly mine and retrain on the frozen 500-record validation set. That would convert it into another known bank."

## 3. Realistic ceiling — can 8,000 synthetic examples reach 70%?

- "Yes, 70% is plausible. No, 8,000 examples alone are not sufficient evidence that it will happen. The real bottleneck is the number of independent semantic constructions, not simply the number of records."
- Fresh-20 has 66 ground-truth section instances over 20 questions ((38+28)/20 = 3.30) — the classifier must recover ~3.3 sections per question with no extra sections. Illustrative sensitivity (P(exact) ≈ r^3.3 × q, q=95%): section recall 80% → 45.5%; 85% → 55.6%; 90% → 67.1% (below target); 93% → 74.8%; 95% → 80.2%. "This is a sensitivity model, not a statistical forecast. Graph v4 produces correlated section outputs... One missed fact may remove three sections simultaneously."
- Planning estimates (subjective, not confidence intervals): 5–8k repetitive synthetic → 45–65%; 5–8k diverse, semantically verified → 60–75%; 12–15k targeted diverse with error mining → 70–85%; 20k repetitive template → potentially still below 70%. "I would not commit to 20,000 records before measuring the first 8,000."
- Next 4,000 records (by graph-sensitive distinctions, from disclosed aggregates): residence/eviction/protection/DV 1,000; Stridhan/joint property/ownership 850; custody/welfare/guardianship 750; divorce/pending proceedings/separation 650; maintenance/enforcement/compensation 450; scope/negation/third-party narratives 300. Target ~40% English, 30% native Kannada, 30% Kanglish, ≥40% multi-issue; minimal-pair counterexamples across all six clusters. Before expanding, train nested subsets of 2,000, 4,000 and 7,500 examples and plot independent development performance vs sample size. "If the curve has already flattened below the target, adding more of the same data is unlikely to solve it."

## 4. Failure modes the diagnosis may be missing

- **4.1 Correlated graph failures.** Itemised FN distribution accounts for 27 of the reported 28 FNs (see §8 inconsistency). The five leading missing section types are 16/28 (57.1%) but should not be read as 16 independent failures — one missed fact can remove several sections. **Run a graph oracle before training:** on independently annotated dev examples, evaluate four configurations: gold issues + gold facts → graph/annotation compatibility; gold issues + predicted facts → fact weakness; predicted issues + gold facts → issue weakness; predicted issues + predicted facts → complete performance. Required oracle result: 100% section exact on unambiguous in-scope examples whose independently assigned section labels are supposed to be supported by Graph v4. If the gold-input oracle fails, the assumption that the recall problem lives entirely in Stage 0/1 is not established; record such failures as a system ceiling, not hidden through incorrect fact predictions. Do not generate the oracle's gold section labels exclusively from Graph v4 (circular).
- **4.2 Named failure modes + mitigations:** multi-issue suppression (independent issue heads; combined-question augmentation); negation leakage (issue-specific scope-aware polarity); mention/request confusion (separate intent states); entity-role confusion (supervised role/entity binding); temporal confusion (temporal-status heads); implicit legal language (layman-language augmentation); cross-clause dependency (contextual full-question branch); property-category confusion (ownership/property-type contrast pairs); silent failures (empty-output diagnostic + evidence recovery); fact co-occurrence shortcut (counterfactual training examples). Diagnostic gates: combined-question requested-issue recall ≥92%; recall drop after combining issues ≤5pp; negation minimal-pair accuracy ≥95%; claimant-role accuracy ≥95%; mention-vs-request accuracy ≥95%; in-scope silent failures ≤1%; graph input contract coverage 100%. "A valid out-of-scope question producing no legal sections is not a silent failure."
- **4.3 Kannada/Kanglish-specific failures.** "Translation loss is only one part of the problem." Agglutinative morphology (inflected forms of ಮನೆ); romanisation variation (nanna, nana, nanage, nange); code-switching (Kannada grammar + English legal terms); dropped subjects (claimant inferred from context); implicit requests (ನನಗೆ ನ್ಯಾಯ ಸಿಗುತ್ತದೆಯಾ?); negation forms (ಇಲ್ಲ, ಬೇಡ, ಮಾಡಿಲ್ಲ); token fragmentation (one colloquial word split into many subwords); informal punctuation (several requests without sentence separators). Build a parallel-language diagnostic with the same semantic scenarios expressed independently in English, Kannada and Kanglish. Targets: ≥90% cross-language section-set agreement; ≥90% recall in each language; max recall difference 5pp between languages. "Accuracy on independently assigned gold labels, because cross-language agreement alone can also mean consistent failure."
- **4.4 Why the 500-record validation set may be misleading.** "The current leakage checks are useful, but insufficient. Zero word-8-gram overlap does not rule out: shared generation templates and scenario families; shared grammar rules; repeated combinations of labels; generator-specific language; semantic similarity expressed with different wording; translation siblings across train and validation. The maximum cosine similarity of 0.8182 is also not interpretable as a definitive leakage guarantee without knowing the similarity model, language behaviour and similarity distribution." Retain the frozen validation split, but audit its provenance. If related scenario families cross the split, record the limitation and create an additional genuinely group-disjoint development set. "Do not use the sealed-bank similarity index repeatedly as a feedback mechanism for training-data optimisation."

## 5. Classifier design — changes before the first training run

- **5.1 Change the default backbone.** "I would not make either proposed English-first large model the default for a three-language legal classifier. ModernBERT-large was pretrained primarily on English and code, while DeBERTa-v3-large is an English-language checkpoint." Candidates: `jhu-clsp/mmBERT-base` (MIT, primary); `microsoft/mdeberta-v3-base` (MIT, multilingual comparison); ModernBERT-large (Apache-2.0, English comparison); DeBERTa-v3-large (MIT, optional English comparison). "mmBERT-base is a 307M-parameter multilingual encoder pretrained across more than 1,800 languages... My first experiment would use mmBERT-base. Before committing, compare tokenizer fragmentation, truncation and extraction performance across the three languages. Verify the exact checkpoint revision and licence before incorporating its weights into production."
- **5.2 Clause-level max-pooling: yes as an initial architecture, not the entire solution.** Full-question only can miss secondary issues; clause voting can suppress rare issues mentioned once; clause max-pooling has strong recall but is vulnerable to isolated false positives; full-question + clause max = recommended baseline; learned contextual fusion = recommended next experiment; complex cross-attention = premature. "The problem with maximum pooling is that the largest score tends to increase as the number of clauses increases, even when no issue is requested." Illustrative (independent per-clause FP probability 5%): P(at least one FP) = 1 − (1−0.05)^n, ~5% at 1 clause rising to 33.7% at 8 clauses. Recommended fusion: c_ij = P(requested_i | C_j, Q); m_i = max_j c_ij; start with s_i = max(p_i^full, m_i). "Calibrate the fused score rather than treating the raw maximum as a calibrated probability." If excessive FPs on longer questions, compare against a lightweight learned fusion: z_i = b_i + w1_i z_i^full + w2_i max_j z_ij + w3_i log(1+n); p_i = σ(z_i). Clauses must have access to surrounding context — a standalone clause can be impossible to interpret if the subject/remedy is in another sentence.
- **5.3 Fix the denied/hypothetical/third-party veto.** "This is the design feature most likely to reintroduce the old recall failure. Do not implement a generic clause-level veto for every issue." Example: "I don't want a divorce, but I want my husband to return my jewellery." — the denied divorce must not suppress the stridhan request, even in the same clause. "Instead, make polarity and modality specific to each issue or fact." Soft-gating baseline: c~_ij = c_ij (1 − g_ij), where g_ij = probability that the particular issue evidence is non-asserted or non-requested. "If the four-state intent head already models these distinctions, avoid penalising the same negative evidence twice. Permit hard suppression only when the evidence is confidently non-requested, scope is unambiguous, and independent affirmative evidence is not suppressed. Do not automatically treat third-party language or hypothetical language as out-of-scope." Gate diagnostics: FN rate introduced by gating ≤2%; positive sibling-clause retention ≥98%; same-clause mixed-polarity accuracy ≥95%; explicit denial suppression ≥95%. "Use a high initial confidence requirement, such as 0.95, for any hard veto. Retain it only if the diagnostic results justify it."
- **5.4 Per-head threshold calibration.** "Use per-head thresholds, but do not independently optimise 39 unrestricted thresholds against only 500 validation questions. Rare heads will not have enough positive and negative examples to support that." Procedure: fit a shared temperature or other low-parameter calibrator to out-of-fold validation predictions; establish a common requested-issue threshold and a separate asserted-fact threshold; search a coarse grid (0.20–0.80 in steps of 0.05); allow head-specific adjustments only for heads with sufficient support; shrink individual thresholds toward the group threshold; optimise downstream Graph v4 exact match subject to precision/recall constraints. Formula: τ_i = [n_i/(n_i+k)] τ̂_i + [k/(n_i+k)] τ_g, k = 50 initial shrinkage constant to evaluate. Heads with <~20 positive calibration examples keep the shared threshold unless independent diagnostics justify a change. Use five-fold grouped cross-validation within the 500 records for calibration assessment. Fit final calibration parameters only after freezing model and calibration choices. "Do not optimise micro-F1 alone. Graph-level exact match is the actual objective, with section precision and recall acting as guardrails."
- **5.5 Most dangerous training details (ranked):** 1. assigning question-level labels to every clause (critical); 2. shared synthetic grammar across splits (critical); 3. missing graph-consumed fact supervision (critical); 4. English-first backbone/tokenizer (high); 5. rare-state and rare-head imbalance (high); 6. silent truncation and clause-splitting errors (high); 7. excessively aggressive negative gating (high); 8. excessive epochs or label smoothing (moderate). Clause supervision: "If a question contains maintenance and custody, do not label every clause as both maintenance and custody. Use the existing spans to assign local supervision where supported. Where clause-level labels are unavailable, mask that loss and rely on question-level supervision." Loss decomposition (starting weights, each group normalised separately): L = L_issue + L_fact + 0.5 L_enum + 0.5 L_clause + 0.2 L_span. "Distinguish explicitly annotated unknown or unasserted states from missing annotations." Class imbalance: maintenance-to-restitution requested ratio 2255/118 ≈ 19.11:1. "Unrestricted inverse-frequency weighting could overcorrect the rare classes and create false positives. Prefer moderate, capped weighting combined with targeted augmentation: w_c = min(3, N_max / N_c). Normalise the weights within each loss group. Evaluate rare-head precision as well as recall." Label smoothing: "Start at zero... Only introduce smoothing if subsequent calibration or overconfidence diagnostics justify it."
- **5.6 RTX 5050, 8 GB training configuration.** "Your proposed batch size of four is potentially misleading. Four questions with eight clauses plus a full-question sequence can result in as many as 36 encoded sequences per forward pass." Recommended: backbone mmBERT-base; BF16 mixed precision; micro-batch 1 question initially; gradient accumulation 8; effective batch 8 questions; lr 2e-5; max epochs 5; checkpoint evaluation every 0.5–1 epoch; gradient clipping 1.0; warmup 5–10%; gradient checkpointing, dynamic padding; memory-efficient AdamW if needed. "If full fine-tuning does not fit, use parameter-efficient fine-tuning or partial layer freezing as a controlled alternative. Do not silently discard information beyond 192 tokens or eight clauses. Record truncation events and use overlapping windows or overflow processing when needed." Training-readiness gates: forward/backward smoke test, record peak GPU memory, verify loss decreases on a small debugging subset, verify no annotated requested issue/fact is removed by truncation in the diagnostic corpus.

## 6. Evaluation design — minimum additional evidence

- "Verdict: 500 synthetic validation questions are necessary but insufficient. They can establish whether: training converges; the classifier learns the intended label structure; rare heads receive meaningful supervision; fusion and calibration improve synthetic performance. They cannot establish reliable accuracy on independently written human questions when the generation process is shared with training."
- Build a **300-question independent human-authored holdout** (no live user data needed; fictional questions by bilingual contributors + independent legal annotators): English 100 → target 80/100; Kannada 100 → 80/100; Kanglish 100 → 80/100; total 300 → 240/300. "Why require 80% rather than 70%? An observed 80/100 gives an approximate two-sided 95% Wilson lower confidence bound of 71.1%. An observed 70/100 gives a lower bound of only approximately 60.4%." Build from the complete legal ontology and graph branch coverage, without sealed questions, their paraphrases, or a category distribution designed to imitate the sealed banks. Authors must not see the training templates; ground-truth annotators must not see classifier predictions; ≥2 independent annotators review legal outputs, disagreements adjudicated before evaluation.
- Go/no-go thresholds (500 synthetic dev vs 300 independent holdout): section exact ≥85% vs ≥80%; micro-section precision ≥93% vs ≥90%; micro-section recall ≥93% vs ≥90%; requested-issue recall ≥95% vs ≥92%; each-language exact ≥80% vs ≥80%; in-scope silent failures ≤1% vs ≤1%; critical role/polarity errors 0 vs 0; graph oracle exact 100% vs 100% on supported unambiguous cases. "The primary go/no-go metric is independent human-authored section exact match, not synthetic validation F1. If the independent holdout is used for model selection or threshold tuning, it ceases to be a locked holdout. A fresh independent final evaluation is then required."
- Sealed-set success criteria: Fresh-20 14/20 (70% exact, net gain of 7); Kannada-8 6/8 (75% exact, net gain of 2). Combined 20/28 = 71.43%. "Even then, the sealed sets are too small to establish a population-level 70% guarantee with strong statistical confidence. In addition, neither supplied sealed set establishes Kanglish accuracy. Since the aggregate sealed results have already informed development priorities, disclose that adaptive exposure when reporting the eventual sealed re-run. Keep the new independent human holdout completely untouched."

## Synthesis — both consultations checked against our own numbers (Muse, 2026-11-27)

Points both models agree on (adopted as the working decision):
1. **Replace Stage 1 with the classifier; regex never a recall gate.** Both verdicts are identical; regex survives only as post-classifier evidence (explicit citations, dates, amounts) + statutory consistency guards.
2. **Multilingual backbone: mmBERT-base (MIT) is the primary candidate** for both; Gemini's alternative is xlm-roberta-large, ChatGPT's is mDeBERTa-v3-base. Both reject English-only ModernBERT-large / DeBERTa-v3-large as the default. Our own check: the Kannada-8 FN pattern (pwdva-18 ×2, Kannada recall 0.70 vs precision 1.00) is consistent with an English-centric encoder — the backbone switch is justified, not just model opinion.
3. **Train the existing 8,000 first, with a learning curve** (nested 2k/4k/7.5k subsets) before committing to expansion. Both agree expansion without diversity is wasted.
4. **Dead fact keys + graph contract first** (P0 in ChatGPT's list, Gemini's Priority 1 audit). Our own check: 8 dead keys were found by the 10x swarm's Lane 01 work — this is a verified fact, not a model guess.
5. **VRAM math:** 36 sequences/step at batch 4 will OOM on 8 GB RTX 5050 — both models independently computed the same number. Adopt bf16 + gradient checkpointing + micro-batch 1 / grad-accum 8; smoke-test peak memory before training.
6. **500-record synthetic validation is not a sufficient gate** — both say it measures in-distribution fit only. Both want an independent human-written holdout before the sealed re-run: Gemini 250-item Blind Stress Set (≥72% exact gate), ChatGPT 300-question holdout (100/language, ≥80% exact per language, Wilson lower bound 71.1%). Adopt the stricter framing: build one independent holdout (300 items, 100/language) and use ≥80% per-language exact as the pre-sealed gate.
7. **Per-head thresholds with shrinkage** (Gemini κ=30, ChatGPT κ=50, both via OOF) — adopt OOF + shrinkage; do not tune 39 free thresholds on 500 rows. No label smoothing (both agree, start at 0.0). Asymmetric/focal loss for imbalance (both agree).
8. **Stop tuning regex anchors** as the primary recall strategy (both lists say this explicitly).

Points where they differ (not settled — needs experiment, not opinion):
- **Can 8k alone reach 70%?** Gemini: no — plateaus 58–66% EN, needs +3–4k LLM paraphrases to cross. ChatGPT: yes, plausibly — 5–8k diverse verified examples → 60–75%, 12–15k targeted diverse → 70–85%; "would not commit to 20k before measuring the first 8,000". Resolution adopted: train the 8k baseline first (with the learning curve), then decide on expansion from the measured curve — ChatGPT's sequencing, not Gemini's pre-commitment to +4k.
- **Next-4,000 allocation:** both give similar totals with different splits (Gemini: 1,100 residence / 900 joint-property+Stridhan / 900 custody / 700 divorce / 400 dense multi-issue; ChatGPT: 1,000 residence / 850 Stridhan+joint / 750 custody / 650 divorce / 450 maintenance / 300 scope-negation). The overlap is the signal: residence/lockout and joint-property/Stridhan are the top two clusters in both. Adopt the union of the two allocations, weighted to residence + joint-property first.
- **Evaluation gate size:** Gemini 250 items / ≥72% overall; ChatGPT 300 items / ≥80% per language. Adopt ChatGPT's (stricter, per-language, statistically grounded in the Wilson bound).
